# Research behind the 2026-09-19 plan

Raw outputs of the planning research (LLM agents, 2026-09-19), kept as evidence. The curated documents are one level up.

- `spike/` — the live control-flow spike: scripts (`*.sps`), runner, raw outputs (`out/`; three runaway-loop logs replaced by head/tail excerpts), the report `spike-control-flow.md`, and the CSUG foreign-interface text it quotes. Run with the chez-libuv incantation in the report. E5d–E5f (added 2026-09-19, evening) reconcile E5c with chez-libuv's threading spike; they were written here directly, so the report's scratchpad paths apply only to E0–E6. E5g (night) re-runs E5c/E5e against chez-libuv v0.6.0, and `out/e5b-thread-direct-uv-write.v060.run{1,2}.txt` re-run E5b there. `smoke-*.sps` (added 2026-09-19, night) are the upstream-landing checks the SDD ledger quotes verbatim: `smoke-upstream` (httparse decisions/methods, uriparse authority-form and host grammar), `smoke-writev` (chez-libuv `uv-write*!`), `smoke-finish` and `smoke-finish-42` (httparse `parser-finish!` for #33/#42), `smoke-v040` (#34 hand-off channels, #36 node-name split), `smoke-uriparse-v030` (uriparse `uri-collector-reset!` for #11, plus the one-shot fresh-vs-reuse measurement), `smoke-uriparse-v040` (uriparse `uri-parse-result-validate` for #18: reset-reused validation equals fresh on eight targets, plus the validated fresh-vs-reuse measurement), `smoke-uriparse-v050` (added 2026-09-24, evening: uriparse's ranged `validate-component` for #21, whose verdict on a span inside a request line equals the verdict on a copy for seven origin-form targets, plus the in-place-vs-copy measurement; its two runs are `out/smoke-uriparse-v050.run{1,2}.txt`), `smoke-uriparse-v060` (added 2026-09-25: uriparse's ranged `validate-authority` and `authority-host-span` for #24, whose verdict and host bounds on a Host value inside its header line equal a copy's for eleven values, plus the in-place-vs-copy measurement; its two runs are `out/smoke-uriparse-v060.run{1,2}.txt`), `smoke-v060-async` (chez-libuv #20: `uv_async` and the `&uv-wrong-thread` affinity refusal), `smoke-v060-tcp` (#21 keepalive and reuseport, `uv-tcp-sockname-address`, and store-and-reraise under the collect-safe `uv-run`); run with the incantation the ledger gives beside each. `m2-event-alloc.sps` (added 2026-09-23, after M2.1 merged) prices M2.1's tagged-list parser events, and M2.1's whole `conn-step` cycle with and without them, against the 3 KiB gate on cps-core's workload. It imports `(http-server connection)`, so it runs on the project's own `make` library path, and its two runs are `out/m2-event-alloc.run{1,2}.txt`. M2.4 (2026-09-25) re-ran `pipeline-pause.sps` at the pins, with the same boundaries as on 2026-09-19 (`out/pipeline-pause.2026-09-25.run{1,2}.txt`), and added `pause-then-upgrade.sps`: with every message's end paused, where a CONNECT's or an Upgrade's hand-off arrives (`out/pause-then-upgrade.run{1,2}.txt`). `pipeline-parse-ahead.sps` (same day, for decision 0004 item 2) prices pausing at every message's end against parsing ahead on 16 pipelined GETs (`out/pipeline-parse-ahead.run{1,2}.txt`). `upgrade-door.sps` (same day, M2.5, for decision 0006's amendment) records where an upgrade's hand-off arrives when on-message-complete answers `continue` and when it answers `pause`, that llhttp's upgrade rule equals the parser's own `upgrade` field, and that the HTTP/2 preface errors before any on-headers-complete (`out/upgrade-door.run{1,2}.txt`). `smoke-httparse-v041.sps` (same day, for chez-httparse #47) records that v0.4.1's documented `upgrade` field, read at each on-headers-complete over the 135 strict fixtures, whole and a byte at a time, equals llhttp's rule and is 1 in exactly the nine fixtures whose logs pause for an upgrade (`out/smoke-httparse-v041.run{1,2}.txt`). `m3-response-costs.sps` (same day, as M3 began, for decision 0010) prices a Date line formatted per response against one the shell keeps, a header safety check, and a prototype serializer on the allocation gate's workload (`out/m3-response-costs.run{1,2}.txt`). `staged-close-*.py` (added 2026-09-26, M3.5, for decision 0011) are Python spikes over loopback, each closing a connection at once or in stages (RFC 9112 §9.6). `staged-close-erase.py` asks whether a reset erases a response the client has already received; it does not, and M3.4's spike had counted a read that succeeded and then met a reset as a failure. `staged-close-linger.py` covers a refusal, a pipelined request that arrives after the close or sits unread, and how long a staged close lasts. `staged-close-truncate.py` covers a handler's 4 MiB response closed at once while a pipelined request sits unread. `staged-close-clients.py` runs curl, Python's http.client and Node's fetch after a 64 KiB head and after a 4 MiB upload is refused. Each ran on Darwin arm64 (Python 3.14.5, curl 8.7.1, Node 20.10.0) and on Linux 6.12.76-linuxkit, in Docker's `debian:trixie` with `python3`, `curl` and `nodejs` installed (Python 3.13.5, curl 8.14.1, Node 20.19.2). The outputs are `out/staged-close-<name>.{darwin,linux}.txt`, from `staged-close-erase.py 100`, `staged-close-linger.py 100`, `staged-close-truncate.py 20` and `staged-close-clients.py 20 curl python node`. `m3-exit-keyword-alloc.sps` (added 2026-09-26, the M3 exit, for byte-machine #139) covers the fields the parser reads by name: each such field alone; a 17-byte name matching more and more of `Transfer-Encoding`; 97 near-miss names in one request; and four requests a server meets, answered through the core as the allocation suite answers them. It runs on the project's `make` library path, or with a patched byte-machine `src` first on it. Its two runs at the old pin are `out/m3-exit-keyword-alloc.run{1,2}.txt`. byte-machine v0.6.1 fixed #139, and its two runs at the new pin, chez-httparse v0.4.3, are `out/m3-exit-keyword-alloc.v043.run{1,2}.txt`. `m4-handler-view.sps` (added 2026-09-26, as M4 began, for what handlers see) prices three views of a request, on top of what parser, adapter and core cost: its spans read through accessors that copy what is asked, every request copied to strings first, and the spans alone. It runs on the project's `make` library path, and its two runs are `out/m4-handler-view.run{1,2}.txt`. `m4-nodelay.sps` and `m4-nodelay.pl` (added 2026-09-26, M4.2's review) time a client on loopback that pipelines sixteen GETs in one write, and one that sends them one at a time, against a server that writes each response once the last write has completed, with `TCP_NODELAY` off and on. The `.sps` drives the shell itself on Darwin (`out/m4-nodelay.darwin.run{1,2}.txt`), setting the option after the adoption so each row measures the mode it names. The `.pl` is the same exchange in Perl, run offline in Docker's `debian:trixie`, which has no Chez (`out/m4-nodelay.linux.run{1,2}.txt`, 10 ms resolution). Neither shows a stall either way. `m4-exit-budget.sps` and `m4-exit-hold.py` (added 2026-09-27, the M4 exit, for design §7.5's per-connection budget) start a server as `serve` does. A Python client, in a process of its own, holds 100 and then 500 keep-alive connections to it, idle after 1, 10, 50 or 200 GETs each, and the server's heap is read for each. The spike then prices a connection's parts, each made fresh. It runs on the project's `make` library path with python3 on PATH (Python 3.14.5), and its two runs are `out/m4-exit-budget.run{1,2}.txt`. `smoke-libuv-v070.sps` (added 2026-09-27, for chez-libuv #28) records v0.7.0's `uv-update-time!`. In a callback that held the loop 100 ms, it moves `uv-now` by the hold and runs nothing. A timer armed after the hold comes due early without it and on time with it. It is refused off the loop's thread, and a million calls are priced beside the workaround the shell carried at v0.6.0, in eight rounds whose order rotates. Each part that fails names how, and the program goes on. It runs on the project's `make` library path, and its two runs are `out/smoke-libuv-v070.run{1,2}.txt`. `m5-exit-budget.sps` (added 2026-09-27, the M5 exit) runs `m4-exit-budget.sps` again on the merged tree, where the shell arms each connection's idle deadline in a wheel entry of its own. Each hold also prints how many connections have a deadline armed and the timers on the loop. It prices the wheel entry, made fresh, and a disarm then an arm, a million times. It runs as its M4 original does, with `m4-exit-hold.py`, and its two runs are `out/m5-exit-budget.run{1,2}.txt`. `m5-exit-copy.sps` (same day, for chez-libuv #35) prices chez-libuv's byte-at-a-time copies, `copy-in!` for a write and `copy-out` for a read, at 16 KiB, 256 KiB, 1 MiB and 4 MiB, beside libc's `memcpy` through one foreign call, and breaks down the work before a 4 MiB answer's write is under way. It runs on the project's `make` library path, and its two runs are `out/m5-exit-copy.run{1,2}.txt`. chez-libuv #35 quotes its first two runs, taken before the 1 MiB row was added. The byte-at-a-time copies' costs there agree with the two recorded within 5 per cent; `memcpy`'s, tens of microseconds, vary more, 4 MiB in 70 to 95 µs there and 95 to 100 µs here. `m5-exit-rcvbuf.sps` (same day, for the socket lane's slow reader) asks how much of an answer the kernel holds on loopback for a client that never reads, with the server's send buffer made 4,096 B: the client's receive buffer left as the kernel makes it, or made 16,384 B after the connect, or before it. Its runs are `out/m5-exit-rcvbuf.darwin.run{1,2}.txt`, and `out/m5-exit-rcvbuf.linux.run{1,2}.txt` on Linux 6.12.76-linuxkit aarch64 in Docker's `chez-libuv-valgrind:10.4.1` image (Debian 12, Chez 10.4.1), built from a copy of the tree.
